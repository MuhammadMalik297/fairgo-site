Privacy policy

The short version: FairGo never sends your data anywhere, and we could not sell it if we wanted to, because we do not have it.

FairGo is published by an independent developer in Australia. Questions about this policy: fairgo.ai.aus@gmail.com.

What FairGo collects

Nothing. FairGo has no account, no server and no analytics, and the app makes no network requests. We receive none of your information.

What you enter is kept on your phone, and used there only to show you your hours, pay checks, tax estimate and budget, and to schedule the reminders you turn on:

  • the details you give when you start: your first name, date of birth, nationality, state, university, course and dates, and whose visa it is;
  • your shifts, jobs, pay rates and course breaks;
  • figures read from pay slips, your tax figures, and your budget;
  • your settings, and small notes the app keeps for itself, such as which notices you have dismissed.

FairGo never sends your data anywhere

Every shift, rate, job and figure you enter is stored on this device. FairGo never sends any of it anywhere.

There is no FairGo account, no login, and no copy of your information on any server of ours — because there is no server.

The app makes no network requests at all. Nothing can be sent because there is nothing to send it to.

We do not sell your data. To anyone. Ever.

Not to employers, not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not to a university, and not to anyone in government.

We could not sell it even if we wanted to — we do not have it.

Nobody is watching what you log

No analytics, no crash tracking that carries your data, no "anonymous usage statistics", no advertising identifiers.

If you go over your 48 hours, the only place that appears is on your own screen.

There is no analytics library in the app, and nothing that reports back.

The Department of Home Affairs cannot see this

FairGo is not connected to Home Affairs, your education provider, the ATO, or Fair Work. It reads their published rules; it does not talk to them about you.

Nothing you log here is reported to anybody.

Pay slips are read on your phone, then FairGo’s copy is deleted

When you photograph a pay slip or pick a PDF, your phone’s own text reader reads it on this device. FairGo keeps the figures it found, such as the hours, rate and pay, and deletes its copy of the photo or file as soon as it has been read.

The original stays wherever you keep it. The camera is used only when you choose to photograph a pay slip or a receipt.

On iPhone the reading is done by Apple’s Vision framework, which works on the device. Nothing is uploaded.

Receipt photos stay inside FairGo on your phone

If you add a photo of a receipt to your deductions, FairGo keeps it inside the app on this device, so it is there at tax time. Nothing reads it and it is never uploaded.

Before it is kept, FairGo removes the details a phone hides inside a photo, such as where and when it was taken and the camera used.

Delete the expense, or use Delete everything, and the photo goes with it.

Data leaves only when you send it

You can build a tax pack to give your tax agent: you pick exactly what goes in it, tick a box to confirm you want to send it, and your phone hands the file to whichever app you choose.

Receipt photos go only if you tick each one, as a PDF of their own, after the same box. FairGo removes hidden details from the photos first, such as where they were taken.

You can also export your records as a PDF or a spreadsheet, to keep or to send to someone you choose. You pick what goes in, and your phone hands it over the same way.

If the pay check finds you may be owed money, Get your money back makes a letter to your employer and a PDF of the calculation. You type your name for it (it is not saved), pick what goes in, tick the same box, and your phone hands it over.

None of them passes through us, and we never see them. Nothing is sent unless you build it and choose where it goes.

Delete it and FairGo keeps nothing

Delete the app and FairGo has no copy of anything. There is no backup of ours anywhere, and nothing to ask us to erase.

To keep the app but remove your record, use Delete everything on this phone in your profile. It removes your shifts, jobs, pay slips, deductions and receipt photos, tax figures and settings, any tax pack or records files FairGo made, and FairGo’s notifications.

Your phone’s own backup is different. If iCloud or Google backup is switched on, it usually includes FairGo’s data, and reinstalling the app or setting up a new phone can bring it back. That copy is in your own account and we cannot read it. You can manage or delete it in your iCloud or Google settings.

That same backup is what keeps your record safe if you lose your phone. With backup off, a lost phone takes the record with it — export your records now and then if you want a copy of your own.

Third parties

FairGo shares your data with no one. It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking services, and no code from another company that reports back. The only time anything leaves your phone is a tax pack you choose to send, to someone you choose, as described above. Your phone’s own backups go to your iCloud or Google account, under Apple’s or Google’s terms.

Keeping, deleting and consent

Your record stays on your phone until you delete it: use Delete everything on this phone in your profile, or delete the app. We hold no copy, so there is nothing to ask us to delete and nothing to request access to.

You give consent each time you send a tax pack, by ticking a box for that pack. There is nothing to withdraw afterwards on our side, because the pack never reaches us; to withdraw it, ask the person you sent it to.

The limits, said plainly

  • Your phone may back itself up to iCloud or Google, and when it does, FairGo’s data is usually part of that. That backup is between you and Apple or Google — we are not involved and cannot read it.
  • Once you share a tax pack, your records or a back pay letter, what happens to them is between you and whoever you sent them to.
  • If the Community tab ever opens, posting there will obviously mean posting in public. It is not open yet, and nothing you have logged would be posted automatically.
  • If you rate FairGo when the App Store asks, the rating goes to Apple and appears on the App Store. FairGo is never told whether you rated it or what you chose.
