How up to date FairGo is

Rules change on two clocks. Award pay rates and tax rates change every year from 1 July, and FairGo is updated for those before they start. Other rules can change at any time, sometimes with only a day’s notice. We check the official sources every week, and when a rule changes we update FairGo as quickly as we can, usually within a few days. Keep automatic updates on, and if FairGo ever differs from the official source, the official source is right.

Questions about FairGo

Is FairGo a government app?

No. FairGo is an independent app. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or acting for the Australian Government or any government agency. It explains the rules that Home Affairs, the Fair Work Ombudsman, the Fair Work Commission and the ATO publish, and every guide names the source it came from.

Is FairGo giving me advice?

No. FairGo works figures out and explains the published rules. It cannot advise on your own visa or tax — a registered migration agent or tax agent can.

Can my employer, my university or Home Affairs see what I log?

No. FairGo is not connected to any of them, and nothing you log is reported to anybody.

What happens to your data

Where is my information kept?

On this phone. FairGo never sends it anywhere. If your phone backs itself up to iCloud or Google, that backup usually includes it — the copy is in your own account and FairGo cannot read it.

What happens to your data

What if I lose my phone?

If your phone’s backup was on, setting up your new phone from it usually brings FairGo’s data back. If it was off, the record is lost — a tax pack made now and then gives you a copy of your own.

How does FairGo count my 48 hours?

Every Monday starts a new two-week window, and FairGo checks all of them. That is why a shift can appear in two fortnights.

Why does FairGo ask about my course?

Your course level decides whether the 48-hour limit applies at all. Research degrees have no limit once they start. FairGo reads the level from the government’s course register, so you do not have to know the term.

Who can help with your pay, visa or tax

Numbers and hours checked on the agencies’ own sites, 29 September to 18 October 2026.

Your pay, or your rights at work

Fair Work Ombudsman

13 13 94, 8 am to 5:30 pm, Monday to Friday

fairwork.gov.au

Free. They help with pay, awards, leave and your rights at work.

You can report a workplace anonymously, in other languages too.

They cannot help with tax or unpaid super — the ATO does those.

Help in your language

Translating and Interpreting Service (TIS National)

131 450, Free

Tell them which language you speak and the number you want to call, such as Fair Work’s 13 13 94. They call for you and interpret.

Fair Work and the Commonwealth Ombudsman both point students to this service.

Free legal advice about work, in NSW

International Student Legal Service NSW (Redfern Legal Centre) (NSW)

02 9698 7645, If they can advise you, a solicitor calls you back

Free and confidential, for international students enrolled anywhere in NSW. Funded by Study NSW and independent of the government.

Underpayment, unpaid wages, losing your job, and housing problems or complaints about your college too.

Need an interpreter? Call TIS National on 131 450 and ask them to call this number.

Free legal advice about work, in Victoria

Study Melbourne Hub (VIC)

1800 056 449, 9 am to 5 pm, Monday to Friday

studymelbourne.vic.gov.au

Free and confidential, for tertiary international students in Victoria. Its legal program gives independent advice on your rights at work and on rental problems.

An interpreter in your language is free. You can also visit at 17 Hardware Lane, Melbourne.

Free legal advice about work, in WA

Circle Green Community Legal (StudyPerth’s legal partner) (WA)

(08) 6148 3636, 9 am to 4 pm, Monday to Friday

StudyPerth’s free legal advice for international students is given by Circle Green.

Pay, leave, losing your job and other problems at work. Bookings are needed, and they put first the workers who cannot easily get help elsewhere.

Reading your enterprise agreement

Fair Work Commission

fwc.gov.au — find an enterprise agreement

Every current agreement is published here. Search the business name or ABN on your pay slip, in quotation marks.

For help with your pay under an agreement, call the Fair Work Ombudsman.

Your tax

Australian Taxation Office

13 28 61, 8 am to 6 pm, Monday to Friday

tpb.gov.au/public-register

For your own situation, use a registered tax agent. Check them on the Tax Practitioners Board register first: generally only registered agents can charge a fee, and the register shows any sanctions.

Free help lodging your tax return

ATO Tax Help (trained volunteers)

13 28 61, Option 2, then option 3. July to October

ato.gov.au — Tax Help

Free if you earned about $70,000 or less and your tax is simple: online, by phone or in person.

Not if you worked as a contractor, such as delivering for an app on an ABN.

Super that has not been paid

Australian Taxation Office — super

13 10 20, 8 am to 6 pm, Monday to Friday

This is the ATO, not Fair Work. Fair Work says it cannot help with unpaid super.

Money is tight

National Debt Helpline

1800 007 007, 9:30 am to 4:30 pm, Monday to Friday

Free, confidential financial counselling. Counsellors do not lend money or sell you anything.

Help with bills, rent and debts: what you can afford, payment plans, and what to pay first.

Your visa

A registered migration agent

mara.gov.au

FairGo can explain the published rules but cannot advise on your own visa. A registered migration agent can.

Check that they are registered on the register at mara.gov.au before you pay anyone.

A problem with your university

National Student Ombudsman

1300 395 775, 10 am to 4 pm Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday; 10 am to 2 pm Wednesday

nso.gov.au

Free and independent. You can complain anonymously by phone.

A private college or education agent

Overseas Student Ombudsman (Commonwealth Ombudsman)

1300 362 072, 10 am to 4 pm Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday; 10 am to 2 pm Wednesday

ombudsman.gov.au

Not a free call from mobiles. The online complaint form is open all hours.

Try your college’s own complaints process first, but do not let being ignored be the end of it.

Your college closed or stopped your course

Tuition Protection Service (Department of Education)

1300 131 798, 9 am to 4 pm, Monday to Friday

education.gov.au/tps

A college that closes must place you in another course or refund the fees you have not used, within 14 days. If it does not, the TPS can help you find another course or get a refund.

Your visa needs you enrolled in a registered course, so act quickly. The TPS cannot help with visas: ask Home Affairs or a registered migration agent.
